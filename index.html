FIELD STATION / 01

A volcano shaped by eruptions.

Mayon is a stratovolcano, a type of volcano built over time from successive deposits of lava, ash, and other volcanic materials. Its steep, broadly symmetrical profile is one of its best-known features.

HISTORICAL FIELD NOTE / 02

Chapters in volcanic history.

A brief overview of selected events in Mayon's recorded history.

CHAPTER 01 · FEBRUARY 1814

The Great Eruption

Mayon's destructive 1814 eruption devastated areas around Cagsawa. The surviving belfry became a lasting historical landmark.

CHAPTER 02 · JUNE 1897

Seven Days of Activity

The 1897 eruption was among the major events in Mayon's documented eruptive history.

CHAPTER 03 · 1993–2000

Modern Eruptive Activity

Eruptions during this period brought renewed attention to monitoring, evacuation planning, and community preparedness.

CHAPTER 04 · 2018–2023

Lava and Summit Dome

Episodes of lava effusion and summit-dome growth were observed during periods of activity.

For current activity, alert levels, and danger-zone information, consult official PHIVOLCS advisories.

CULTURAL MONOGRAPH

The Legend of Daragang Magayon.

In Bicolano oral tradition, “Magayon” means beautiful. The volcano is associated with the story of the maiden Daragang Magayon and her beloved Panganoron. Versions of the legend may differ across tellings.

LANDMARK / MONUMENT

Cagsawa Belfry & Mayon

Daraga, Albay · Philippines

The Cagsawa Ruins are a familiar heritage site in Albay. The remaining church belfry is often photographed with Mayon Volcano in the background.

FIELD NOTE / SAFETY

Respect the mountain.

Mayon is an active volcano. Conditions can change, so visitors and residents should follow instructions from local authorities and official monitoring agencies.

Before visiting

  • Check the latest PHIVOLCS Mayon updates and alert level.
  • Observe official danger-zone restrictions and evacuation orders.
  • Follow guidance from local disaster-risk-reduction offices.

FIELD STATION / NATURAL ENVIRONMENT

Life on the volcanic slopes.

The landscapes around Mayon include forested areas and habitats shaped by elevation, climate, and volcanic soils. Protecting these environments helps preserve the region's natural heritage.

Explore responsibly

Stay on permitted paths, avoid disturbing plants and wildlife, and follow protected-area rules and local guidance.
